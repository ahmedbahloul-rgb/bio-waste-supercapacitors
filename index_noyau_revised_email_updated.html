The situation

Two problems, one solution.

The energy transition needs fast, durable energy storage. At the same time, agricultural residues represent an underused source of carbon-rich feedstock.

The energy challenge

  • Conventional batteries hit limits on power delivery and cycle life
  • Supercapacitors offer rapid charge/discharge and exceptional durability
  • Activated carbon, the key raw material, remains costly and largely imported

The Algerian context

  • Algeria has important date and olive production, generating valuable agricultural residues
  • These residues can be converted into higher-value carbon materials instead of remaining underutilized
  • Scientific literature confirms the potential of these residues as activated-carbon precursors
From biomass to materials

Waste streams become a new energy resource.

Our concept connects agricultural residues with advanced carbon materials: collect the biomass, convert it into porous carbon, engineer the electrode, and validate electrochemical performance.

The process

From pit to electrode, in four steps.

A locally available biomass feedstock transformed through a controlled and scalable materials-processing route.

01 / COLLECTION

Collection & crushing

Date and olive pits sourced from local agricultural supply chains, then crushed to a fine particle size.

02 / CARBONIZATION

Thermal treatment

High-temperature pyrolysis under controlled atmosphere to produce a carbon-rich char.

03 / ACTIVATION

Chemical activation (KOH)

Creation of a microscopic pore network that multiplies the carbon's active surface area.

04 / FABRICATION

Electrode assembly

Shaping the activated carbon into electrodes ready to integrate into a supercapacitor.

Scientific validation

Strong laboratory performance.

Our results include binary/ternary carbon materials based on date pits, olive pits and sugar, as well as hybrid electrodes combining olive-derived activated carbon with polyaniline (PAni) and reduced graphene oxide (rGO).
